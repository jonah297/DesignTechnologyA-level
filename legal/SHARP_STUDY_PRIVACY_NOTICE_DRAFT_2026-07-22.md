## 3. Key Definitions

In this Privacy Notice:

- **App** means the Sharp Study web application, website, dashboards, tools, content, services, features, and related documentation.

- **Student Performance Data** means educational data generated through the App, including topic progress, assignments, scores, mastery, attempts, timestamps, learning activity, streaks, XP, and support indicators.
- **Personal data** means information relating to an identified or identifiable living person.

## 4. Our Role Under Data-Protection Law

The exact data-protection role may depend on how Sharp Study is being used.

### 4.1 Independent Student Use

Where a student uses Sharp Study independently and not through a school or teacher-linked account, Sharp Study will usually decide why and how the student's account and learning data are processed. In that situation, Sharp Study is likely to act as a controller for that personal data.

### 4.2 School-Linked Use

Where a school or institution decides to use Sharp Study with its students, the school or institution may decide the purpose of using the App for those students. In that situation:

- the school or institution may act as controller for school-linked student use;
- Sharp Study may act as processor for parts of the service that it provides on the school's instructions;
- Sharp Study may also act as an independent controller for some limited purposes, such as account administration, platform security, legal compliance, product feedback, billing, service improvement, and aggregated analytics.

The exact controller/processor position should be confirmed in the school pilot agreement or data-processing agreement.

### 4.3 Teacher and Institution Use

For Teacher User accounts, Account Manager accounts, school-contact data, support communications, access codes, billing information, and platform-security data, Sharp Study may act as controller because it decides how that data is used to provide, secure, manage, and improve the App.

## 8. Lawful Bases for Processing

The lawful basis depends on the purpose and context.

### 8.1 Contract

We may process personal data where it is necessary to provide the App under our Terms and Conditions, pilot arrangement, subscription, or licence.

Examples include creating accounts, logging in, providing study tools, managing class access, and delivering features requested by users.

### 8.2 Legitimate Interests

We may process personal data where it is necessary for legitimate interests, provided those interests are not overridden by user rights and freedoms.

Examples may include:

- keeping the App secure;
- preventing misuse;
- improving the App;
- responding to support requests;
- using aggregated insights to understand product performance;
- maintaining business records;
- protecting our legal rights.

Where children are involved, we will consider their best interests and the need for additional protection.

### 8.3 Consent

We may rely on consent for optional activities, such as optional marketing, optional research participation, or optional features where consent is the most appropriate lawful basis.

Where consent is used, users may withdraw consent at any time. Withdrawal does not affect processing already carried out before consent was withdrawn.

For school-linked student use, consent may not always be the most appropriate lawful basis because students may not have a genuine free choice where a school directs or strongly encourages use. School authorisation and the school's lawful basis may be relevant instead.

### 8.4 Legal Obligation

We may process personal data where needed to comply with a legal obligation, such as responding to valid legal requests, keeping tax or accounting records, or handling data-protection rights.

### 8.5 Public Task or Education-Related Basis Used by Schools

Schools and other public education bodies may rely on their own lawful bases, such as public task or legal obligation, when they decide to use edtech tools with students. Sharp Study does not decide the school's lawful basis for school-controlled use.

### 8.6 Special Category Data

Sharp Study is not designed to collect special category data, such as health data, ethnicity, religion, biometric data, political opinions, or safeguarding records.

Users should not enter unnecessary sensitive information into Sharp Study. If special category data is accidentally submitted, we may delete, restrict, or handle it according to applicable law, school instructions, and safety requirements.

## 9. Student Data Visibility

### 9.1 Independent Student Users

If you are an Independent Student User, your individual study progress is not shown to a teacher dashboard unless you later join a class or school-linked account.

Sharp Study may still use your data to provide the App, keep your account secure, show your own progress, improve the service, and comply with legal obligations.

### 9.2 Linked Student Users

If you join a class, school, institution, teacher, or licence, authorised Teacher Users and Account Managers may see Student Performance Data connected to that class or institution.

This may include:

- your name or display name;
- your school email address where needed for class management or identity checks;
- the class or classes you have joined;
- topics attempted;
- mastery, scores, progress, and answer outcomes;
- assignment status, deadlines, attempts, completion, and timestamps;
- last active indicators and study activity;
- support reminders, reward messages, and nudge indicators;
- leaderboard rank where enabled.

### 9.3 Why Teachers See This Data

Teachers see this data so they can monitor educational progress, set appropriate work, provide support, identify topics that need reteaching, check assignment completion, and help students learn.

Teachers and schools must use this data in line with their own school policies, safeguarding duties, data-protection obligations, and professional judgement.

### 9.4 Teacher Decisions

Sharp Study provides learning indicators. Teachers and schools remain responsible for decisions they make using those indicators.

Sharp Study data should not be treated as the sole evidence for serious disciplinary, safeguarding, academic, or welfare decisions.
